# Scrum

## 1. What is Scrum

**Scrum** is an agile framework used to develop, deliver, and maintain complex products. It provides a simple structure based on roles, events, and artifacts that help teams work collaboratively, efficiently, and adaptively.

Unlike a traditional methodology, Scrum does not prescribe exactly how work should be performed. Instead, it defines a set of rules that enables the team to determine the best way to achieve its goals.

Scrum is widely used in software development, but it can also be applied in areas such as marketing, education, human resources, engineering, and project management.

---

## 2. Scrum Objectives

Scrum aims to:

- Deliver value continuously to customers;
- Adapt quickly to change;
- Improve collaboration among team members;
- Reduce risks through frequent deliveries;
- Promote continuous improvement.

---

## 3. The Three Pillars of Scrum

Scrum is based on **Empiricism**, which states that knowledge comes from experience and decision-making based on observation.

Empiricism is supported by three pillars.

### 3.1 Transparency

All important information should be visible to everyone involved.

Examples include:

- An accessible Product Backlog;
- An up-to-date Sprint Backlog;
- Clear quality criteria.

Without transparency, it becomes impossible to make good decisions.

### 3.2 Inspection

Work progress should be inspected frequently to identify problems as early as possible.

Inspection takes place during all Scrum events, especially during:

- Daily Scrum;
- Sprint Review;
- Sprint Retrospective.

### 3.3 Adaptation

When a problem is identified during an inspection, the team should quickly adjust its process or plan.

This minimizes waste and increases the likelihood of project success.

---

## 4. The Five Scrum Values

Scrum is also based on five core values.

1. **Commitment:** The team commits to the Sprint Goal and works together to achieve it.

2. **Focus:** During the Sprint, the team concentrates only on the planned work.

3. **Openness:** Everyone shares information transparently, including challenges, risks, and progress.

4. **Respect:** Each team member recognizes the skills and contributions of others.

5. **Courage:** The team should have the courage to:

- Admit mistakes;
- Suggest improvements;
- Face problems;
- Experiment with new solutions.

---

## 5. Scrum Team (Scrum Roles)

Scrum defines only **three official roles**, collectively known as the **Scrum Team**.

There is no project manager in Scrum.

The team is responsible for organizing its own work.

A Scrum Team should be:

- Self-managing;
- Cross-functional;
- Collaborative;
- Responsible for quality.

### 5.1 Product Owner (PO)

The **Product Owner** is responsible for maximizing the value of the product.

They represent the customer's interests and ensure that the team builds what truly delivers value.

#### 5.1.1 Responsibilities

- Define the product vision;
- Create and maintain the Product Backlog;
- Prioritize backlog items;
- Clarify questions from the team;
- Validate deliveries.

#### 5.1.2 What a Good Product Owner Does

- Frequently communicates with customers;
- Understands business needs;
- Prioritizes important features;
- Makes decisions quickly.

#### 5.1.3 What They Do Not Do

- They do not manage people;
- They do not assign tasks;
- They do not control the team's working hours.

### 5.2 Scrum Master

The **Scrum Master** is the Scrum facilitator.

Their primary goal is to ensure that the Scrum framework is properly followed and to remove obstacles that hinder the team's work.

They act as a **Servant Leader**.

#### 5.2.1 Responsibilities

- Teach Scrum to the team;
- Facilitate Scrum events;
- Remove impediments;
- Protect the team from external interference;
- Promote continuous improvement.

#### 5.2.2 Examples of Impediments

- Development environment unavailable;
- Lack of access to required tools;
- Dependency on another team;
- Communication issues.

#### 5.2.3 What the Scrum Master Does NOT Do

- They are not the team's boss;
- They do not assign tasks;
- They do not monitor individual productivity.

### 5.3 Developers

**Developers** are the professionals responsible for building the product increment.

Despite the name, they are not limited to software developers.

They may include:

- Software Developers;
- Testers;
- Designers;
- Business Analysts;
- Database Specialists;
- DevOps Engineers;
- UX/UI Designers.

#### 5.3.1 Responsibilities

- Plan Sprint work;
- Develop features;
- Test the product;
- Ensure quality;
- Deliver a Done Increment.

## 6. Scrum Artifacts

Artifacts represent the work performed by the team and ensure transparency throughout development.

Scrum defines **three main artifacts**.

### 6.1 Product Backlog

The **Product Backlog** is an ordered list of everything that may be developed for the product.

It is the responsibility of the Product Owner.

Items are typically written as **User Stories**.

#### 6.1.1 Example

| Priority | Item                     |
| -------- | ------------------------ |
| 1        | User login               |
| 2        | Password recovery        |
| 3        | Customer registration    |
| 4        | Administrative dashboard |

The Product Backlog is never complete.

It continuously evolves.

### 6.2 Sprint Backlog

The **Sprint Backlog** contains the items selected for the current Sprint.

In addition to the selected items, it also includes the plan for implementing them.

It is owned by the Developers.

During the Sprint, the Sprint Backlog may change as the team learns more about the work.

### 6.3 Increment

The **Increment** represents everything completed during the Sprint.

It must be:

- Working;
- Integrated into the product;
- Tested;
- Ready for use.

If the Increment is not truly complete, it cannot be considered done.

---

## 7. Definition of Done (DoD)

Although it is not an artifact, the **Definition of Done** is a commitment associated with the Increment.

It defines when an item can truly be considered complete.

Example:

- Code implemented;
- Tests executed;
- Code review completed;
- Documentation updated;
- Deployment completed.

---

## 8. Scrum Events

Scrum events ensure inspection, adaptation, and transparency throughout development.

All events have a maximum duration (**time-box**).

### 8.1 Sprint

The Sprint is the heart of Scrum.

It is a fixed period during which the team develops a product increment.

A Sprint typically lasts:

- 1 week;
- 2 weeks;
- 3 weeks;
- Up to 1 month.

At the end of the Sprint, there should be a potentially usable Increment.

### 8.2 Sprint Planning

Sprint Planning is the meeting that starts the Sprint.

During this meeting, the team answers three questions:

1. What value will be delivered?
2. What will be developed?
3. How will the work be accomplished?

At the end of the meeting, the following are defined:

- Sprint Goal;
- Sprint Backlog.

### 8.3 Daily Scrum

The Daily Scrum is a daily meeting lasting up to **15 minutes**.

Its purpose is to synchronize the team's work and adjust the plan.

The Daily Scrum is not intended for reporting status to the Scrum Master.

Instead, it enables the Developers to organize their own work.

A common format is to answer:

- What did I do yesterday?
- What will I do today?
- Are there any impediments?

### 8.4 Sprint Review

The Sprint Review takes place at the end of the Sprint.

During this event, the team presents the completed Increment to stakeholders.

Objectives include:

- Demonstrate completed features;
- Receive feedback;
- Update the Product Backlog;
- Discuss next steps.

It is not simply a meeting to showcase screens.

It is an opportunity to inspect the product.

### 8.5 Sprint Retrospective

The Sprint Retrospective takes place after the Sprint Review.

Its goal is to continuously improve the team's way of working.

The team discusses:

- What went well?
- What did not go well?
- What can be improved in the next Sprint?

At the end, improvement actions are usually defined for the next Sprint.

## 11. Best Practices

- Keep the Product Backlog up to date.
- Clearly define the Definition of Done.
- Avoid constant changes during the Sprint.
- Conduct all Scrum events.
- Encourage open communication among team members.
- Deliver working software at the end of every Sprint.
- Pursue continuous improvement through the Sprint Retrospective.

---

## 12. Conclusion

Scrum is a simple yet highly effective framework for product development. Its structure, based on **roles**, **artifacts**, and **events**, enables teams to work in an organized, collaborative, and adaptable way.

By focusing on frequent deliveries, continuous inspection, and ongoing improvement, Scrum reduces risks, increases transparency, and delivers value to customers and organizations incrementally.
