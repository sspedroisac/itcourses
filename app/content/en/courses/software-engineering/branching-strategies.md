# Branching Strategies

Branching strategies define how a team organizes parallel work in version control.

The right strategy depends on team size, release process, and risk tolerance.

---

## Why Branching Strategy Matters

Without a shared approach, teams may run into:

- Merge conflicts.
- Confusing release workflows.
- Unclear ownership of changes.
- Delayed integration.

A strategy creates predictability.

---

## Common Approaches

Common branching approaches include:

- Short-lived feature branches.
- Trunk-based development.
- Release branches.
- Hotfix branches.

Some teams keep branches very short to encourage frequent integration.

---

## Tradeoffs

Long-lived branches can reduce short-term interference but often increase merge difficulty.

Frequent integration reduces divergence but requires stronger automation and discipline.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Branching strategy shapes how teams integrate and release work.
- Shorter feedback loops often reduce integration pain.
- The best strategy is the one the team can apply consistently.
- Branching should support delivery, not become a source of delay.
