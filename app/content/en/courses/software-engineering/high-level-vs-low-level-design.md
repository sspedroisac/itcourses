# High-Level vs Low-Level Design

Software design can be viewed at different levels of detail.

High-level design focuses on the broad structure of the system, while low-level design focuses on the internal details of components.

---

## High-Level Design

High-level design answers questions such as:

- What major components exist?
- How do they communicate?
- Where is data stored?
- What external systems are involved?

This level is useful for architecture discussions and planning.

---

## Low-Level Design

Low-level design addresses details such as:

- Class or module responsibilities.
- Function boundaries.
- Validation rules.
- Error handling.
- Internal data structures.

This level is closer to actual implementation.

---

## Why Both Matter

Teams need both views.

If they only think at a high level, implementation may become inconsistent.

If they only think at a low level, they may miss broader system consequences.

---

## Key Takeaways

- High-level design describes the major structure of a system.
- Low-level design describes detailed implementation decisions.
- Both perspectives are necessary for coherent software engineering.
- Good teams know when each level of discussion is appropriate.
