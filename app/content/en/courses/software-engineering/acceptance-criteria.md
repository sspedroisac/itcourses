# Acceptance Criteria

Acceptance criteria define the conditions that must be true for a requirement or user story to be considered complete.

They turn broad ideas into testable expectations.

---

## Why Acceptance Criteria Matter

Without acceptance criteria, people may interpret the same story differently.

Acceptance criteria help teams align on:

- Expected behavior.
- Edge cases.
- Business rules.
- Scope boundaries.

They reduce ambiguity before development begins.

---

## Example

For a password reset feature, acceptance criteria might include:

- Users can request a reset by email.
- The reset link expires after a set period.
- Invalid or expired links display a clear error.
- A successful reset allows the user to sign in with the new password.

These points are easier to test than a vague story alone.

---

## Characteristics of Good Criteria

Strong acceptance criteria are:

- Specific.
- Testable.
- Relevant to the user story.
- Clear enough for shared understanding.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Acceptance criteria define what done means.
- They reduce ambiguity and support testing.
- Good criteria make expectations visible before coding starts.
- Clear criteria improve collaboration between product, engineering, and QA.
