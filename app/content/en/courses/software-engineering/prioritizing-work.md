# Prioritizing Work

Software teams almost always have more ideas than time.

Prioritization is the process of deciding what to build first and what can wait.

---

## Factors That Influence Priority

Teams often weigh factors such as:

- User value.
- Business impact.
- Risk reduction.
- Technical dependencies.
- Effort.
- Deadlines or compliance needs.

Not all important work has the same kind of value.

---

## Short-Term vs Long-Term Value

Some tasks deliver immediate business benefit, while others reduce future cost.

Examples of long-term value include:

- Refactoring unstable code.
- Improving test coverage.
- Upgrading critical dependencies.

If teams only prioritize visible features, the product may become harder to change.

---

## Common Prioritization Methods

Teams may use:

- MoSCoW (Must, Should, Could, Won't).
- Impact vs effort comparisons.
- Weighted scoring.
- Strategic roadmap alignment.

The exact method matters less than making tradeoffs explicit.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Prioritization helps teams focus limited time on the highest-value work.
- Good prioritization considers both immediate features and long-term health.
- Technical work can be strategically important even when users do not see it directly.
- Clear tradeoffs are better than accidental drift.
