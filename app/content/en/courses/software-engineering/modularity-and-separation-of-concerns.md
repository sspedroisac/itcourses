# Modularity and Separation of Concerns

Modularity means organizing software into smaller parts with clear responsibilities.

Separation of concerns means keeping different kinds of logic isolated from each other.

---

## Why It Matters

When code is highly tangled, changes become risky.

Good modular design helps teams:

- Understand code faster.
- Test pieces independently.
- Reuse components.
- Reduce unintended side effects.

---

## Examples

Examples of separated concerns include:

- Keeping business rules separate from UI code.
- Isolating database access from request handling.
- Separating validation from persistence.

These boundaries make systems easier to maintain.

---

## Warning Signs

Poor separation of concerns often appears as:

- Very large files.
- Functions doing many unrelated tasks.
- Hidden dependencies.
- Repeated logic across modules.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Modularity makes software easier to understand and change.
- Separation of concerns reduces coupling between unrelated responsibilities.
- Strong boundaries improve testing, maintenance, and team collaboration.
- Good structure lowers the cost of future changes.
