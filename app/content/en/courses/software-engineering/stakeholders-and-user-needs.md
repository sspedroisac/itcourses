# Stakeholders and User Needs

Software is built for people, organizations, and processes. To build the right solution, teams must understand the needs of their stakeholders.

---

## Who Are Stakeholders?

Stakeholders are people or groups affected by the software.

They may include:

- End users.
- Customers.
- Business sponsors.
- Support teams.
- Developers and testers.
- Compliance or security teams.

Each stakeholder may have different priorities.

---

## Understanding User Needs

User needs describe what people are trying to accomplish.

Instead of asking only for features, teams should ask:

- What task is the user trying to complete?
- What problem is frustrating them today?
- What does success look like from their perspective?

This helps avoid building features that sound useful but do not solve the real problem.

---

## Conflicting Priorities

Stakeholders do not always agree.

For example:

- Users may want fewer steps.
- Security teams may want stronger verification.
- Business leaders may want faster delivery.

Software engineering involves balancing these pressures thoughtfully.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Stakeholders are everyone affected by a system.
- User needs should be understood in terms of real goals and problems.
- Good software comes from listening carefully and clarifying tradeoffs.
- Engineering decisions improve when stakeholder perspectives are explicit.
