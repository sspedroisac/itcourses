# Choosing the Right Database

There is no single best database for every system.

Choosing the right one depends on the data model, workload, reliability needs, team skills, and operational constraints.

---

## Questions to Ask

Teams should ask questions such as:

- Is the data highly relational?
- Are transactions critical?
- Is the workload read-heavy or write-heavy?
- How important are scale and global distribution?
- What operational complexity can the team handle?

The answers help narrow the right fit.

---

## Avoiding Trend-Driven Decisions

Database choices should be based on problem fit, not popularity alone.

A familiar, reliable relational database is often the best option unless the workload clearly demands something else.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Database choice is a tradeoff, not a popularity contest.
- Workload, consistency, scale, and team capability all matter.
- Simpler systems are often easier to operate well.
- Good technology selection starts with problem understanding.
