# Key-Value Databases

Key-value databases store data as pairs made of a unique key and an associated value.

They are often optimized for very fast lookups.

---

## Typical Use Cases

Key-value systems are commonly used for:

- Caching.
- Session storage.
- Feature flags.
- Simple high-speed retrieval.

They are most effective when access is centered on direct lookup by key.

---

## Limitations

Key-value stores are usually less suitable for complex relational queries or rich analytical patterns.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Key-value databases are simple and fast for direct lookups.
- They work well for caching and lightweight state storage.
- Their query model is often more limited than relational systems.
- They are best used for workloads that match their strengths.
