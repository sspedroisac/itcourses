# Requirements Gathering Techniques

Requirements gathering is the process of discovering what a system should do and what constraints it must satisfy.

Strong requirements reduce confusion, rework, and missed expectations.

---

## Common Techniques

Teams gather requirements using methods such as:

- Interviews.
- Workshops.
- Surveys.
- Observation.
- Reviewing existing systems.
- Prototyping.

Different projects benefit from different combinations.

---

## Interviews and Workshops

Interviews are useful when you need detailed input from specific stakeholders.

Workshops are better when multiple people must align on priorities, constraints, and terminology.

Both techniques work best when questions focus on outcomes, pain points, and business rules.

---

## Observation and Existing Workflows

Users often describe their work differently from how they actually perform it.

Observing real workflows can reveal:

- Hidden steps.
- Manual workarounds.
- Common delays.
- Frequent mistakes.

This helps teams build software that fits real behavior.

---

## Prototypes

Simple prototypes can clarify requirements faster than long documents.

They help stakeholders react to something concrete, which often surfaces missing assumptions.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Good requirements come from active discovery, not guesswork.
- Interviews, workshops, observation, and prototypes are common tools.
- The best technique depends on the project and stakeholders.
- Early clarification reduces expensive rework later.
