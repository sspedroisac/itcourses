# The Software Development Lifecycle

The **Software Development Lifecycle (SDLC)** describes the stages a team follows to plan, build, release, and maintain software.

While organizations use different names and processes, the underlying flow is similar.

---

## Common Stages

Most software projects include stages such as:

- Planning.
- Requirements analysis.
- Design.
- Implementation.
- Testing.
- Deployment.
- Maintenance.

These stages may happen sequentially or iteratively depending on the development approach.

---

## Why the SDLC Is Useful

The SDLC gives teams a shared structure for organizing work.

It helps answer questions like:

- What problem are we solving?
- What should be built first?
- How will quality be checked?
- How will changes be released safely?

Without a clear lifecycle, projects often become reactive and chaotic.

---

## Iterative Development

Modern teams rarely complete all planning before writing code.

Instead, they often work in short iterations:

- Define a small set of goals.
- Implement a slice of functionality.
- Test and review it.
- Release or validate it.
- Learn from feedback.

This allows teams to adapt as they learn more.

---

## Maintenance Is Part of the Lifecycle

Software work does not end after deployment.

Teams must continue to:

- Fix bugs.
- Improve performance.
- Update dependencies.
- Add new features.
- Respond to incidents.

Much of software engineering happens after the first release.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The SDLC is a structured view of how software is built and maintained.
- Common stages include planning, design, development, testing, deployment, and maintenance.
- Modern teams usually work iteratively rather than in a single long sequence.
- Successful software engineering treats maintenance as an ongoing responsibility.
