# Types of Databases

Not all databases are designed for the same kind of work.

Different database types exist because applications have different data shapes, scale requirements, and consistency needs.

---

## Common Categories

Some common database categories include:

- Relational databases.
- Document databases.
- Key-value databases.
- Column-family databases.
- Graph databases.

Each type is optimized for different use cases.

---

## Relational Databases

Relational databases store data in tables and use SQL for querying.

They are strong when you need:

- Structured data.
- Relationships between entities.
- Transaction support.
- Clear constraints and consistency.

---

## Non-Relational Databases

Non-relational databases, often grouped under NoSQL, support more flexible data models or different scaling patterns.

They may be better suited for:

- Rapidly changing schemas.
- Very large distributed workloads.
- Highly connected data.
- Simple high-speed lookups.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Different database types solve different problems.
- Relational systems are strong for structured and consistent data.
- NoSQL systems offer alternative tradeoffs in flexibility and scale.
- Good database choices depend on the system's needs, not trends alone.
