# Structured Data and Unstructured Data

Data can be organized in different ways.

Understanding the difference between structured and unstructured data helps explain why different storage systems exist.

---

## Structured Data

Structured data follows a defined format.

Examples include:

- Customer records.
- Product prices.
- Bank transactions.
- Inventory counts.

This kind of data fits well into rows and columns.

---

## Unstructured Data

Unstructured data does not fit neatly into fixed tables.

Examples include:

- Images.
- Videos.
- Documents.
- Audio files.
- Large free-text content.

This data may still have metadata, but its internal content is less uniform.

---

## Semi-Structured Data

Some data sits between the two extremes.

Formats such as JSON and XML are often called semi-structured because they allow flexible fields while still preserving some organization.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Structured data follows a fixed format and works well in relational systems.
- Unstructured data is less uniform and may require different storage approaches.
- Semi-structured formats offer flexibility with some organizational structure.
- Data shape strongly influences database design decisions.
