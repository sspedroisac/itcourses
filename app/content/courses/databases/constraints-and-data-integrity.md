# Constraints and Data Integrity

Constraints are rules enforced by the database to protect data quality.

They help ensure that stored information remains valid and consistent.

---

## Common Constraint Types

Typical constraints include:

- Primary key constraints.
- Foreign key constraints.
- Unique constraints.
- Not-null constraints.
- Check constraints.

Each one prevents a different category of bad data.

---

## Why Integrity Matters

Without integrity rules, a database may accept:

- Duplicate identifiers.
- Missing required values.
- Invalid relationships.
- Impossible business states.

These issues can spread throughout an application.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Constraints protect database integrity.
- Integrity means data remains valid, consistent, and trustworthy.
- Important rules should be enforced at the database level when appropriate.
- Reliable systems depend on strong data guarantees.
